TASK 1 — ESTIMATE HOUSING PRICES NEAR TRANSIT
Dataset: UCI Real Estate Valuation (414 transactions).
Feature: distance to nearest MRT station divided by 100 (distance_100m).
Target: house price of unit area, in units of 10,000 New Taiwan dollars per ping.

(a) Linear regression:
Slope = -0.7262 price units per 100 m
Intercept = 45.8514 price units

(b) At 500 m, distance_100m = 5:
Predicted price = 42.2204 price units.

(c) Interpretation:
The slope is negative. For every additional 100 m from the nearest MRT station,
the fitted price decreases by about 0.726 price units per ping,
equivalent to about 7,262 New Taiwan dollars per ping.
This describes the fitted association in this dataset, not proof that distance
alone causes the price difference. The scatter plot and fitted line are saved as
task1_housing.png.

TASK 2 — COMPARE MODELS FOR YACHT RESISTANCE
Dataset: UCI Yacht Hydrodynamics (308 experiments).
Feature: sixth column (index 5), Froude number.
Target: seventh column (index 6), residuary resistance per unit displacement weight.
Validation: same shuffled five folds, random_state=42, scoring=negative RMSE.

Linear fold RMSEs: [8.16, 10.57, 9.84, 8.38, 7.78]
Linear mean CV RMSE: 8.95

Quadratic fold RMSEs: [3.93, 4.59, 4.45, 4.49, 3.5]
Quadratic mean CV RMSE: 4.19

Decision: The quadratic model is better because its mean RMSE (4.19)
is lower than the linear model's (8.95). Lower RMSE means smaller
prediction errors. As noted in the lab handout, a row-based split can place
experiments from the same hull in different folds.

TASK 3 — TUNE A CONCRETE STRENGTH MODEL
Dataset: UCI Concrete Compressive Strength. Exact duplicate rows were removed.
Rows after duplicate removal: 1005
Training rows: 804
Test rows: 201

(a) Linear baseline mean five-fold CV RMSE: 10.20 MPa
Fold RMSEs: [10.1, 10.18, 10.05, 10.18, 10.49]

(b) Top three settings ranked by GridSearchCV (mean CV RMSE):
Rank 1: degree=3, alpha=0.1, mean CV RMSE=6.09 MPa\nRank 2: degree=3, alpha=0.01, mean CV RMSE=6.16 MPa\nRank 3: degree=3, alpha=1, mean CV RMSE=6.27 MPa\n
Best parameters: {'poly__degree': 3, 'ridge__alpha': 0.1}
Best mean CV RMSE: 6.09 MPa

(c) Held-out test performance of the refitted winner:
Test RMSE: 6.52 MPa
Test MAE: 4.67 MPa
Actual-versus-predicted plot saved as task3_concrete.png.

(d) Comparison and explanation:
The tuned model's mean CV RMSE (6.09 MPa) is lower than
the linear baseline (10.20 MPa), indicating improved
cross-validation prediction accuracy. One degree-2 interaction feature is
Cement × Water. PolynomialFeatures creates interaction terms between input
ingredients. StandardScaler is inside the pipeline so scaling is fitted
separately on each training fold during cross-validation; this avoids leaking
validation-fold information into preprocessing. Scaling also puts features on
comparable scales before Ridge penalizes coefficient magnitudes.
